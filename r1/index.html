An Invitation

Bret — what you hold is rare. Not just acreage, but position: eighty contiguous acres on the Rim, wrapped on every side by national forest, minutes from two of Arizona's finest golf communities, and an easy drive from the entire Phoenix valley seeking elevation, pine air, and a place that feels like nowhere else in the state.

This is our case that Granite Rim Reserve can become the high country's answer to Scottsdale's Sanctuary and Tucson's Ritz-Carlton at Dove Mountain — a destination resort of casitas, villas, and residences, with a world-class spa, distinctive dining, and an outdoor playground most resorts can only photograph.

We are not here to sell you on leaving. We are here to show you what your land could be — and to invite a conversation about building it together.

The Captive-Destination Thesis

Dove Mountain sits in remote desert with nowhere to wander off to — so guests spend the entire stay on property.

That captures dining, spa, and experience revenue a city resort leaks to the surrounding town. Payson is the same setup — the Rim is the draw, not the strip mall. The Reserve should be built to own the whole guest wallet: every meal, treatment, ride, and celebration happens here. It also argues for dedicated event & meeting space — intimate weddings and corporate retreats that book the property and fill mid-week rooms.

The Path

The real work — and how we get there.

We are not naïve about the obstacles. Each is a known, solvable step — and we've thought about who carries it.
01

Zoning & Entitlement

Rezone / conditional-use through Gila County for resort-residential use. A planned-area-development approach lets us phase density and preserve the forest edge.

Lead: Land-use counsel + County Planning
02

Utilities & Water

Securing water, wastewater, and power to a Rim-country parcel is the critical path. Options span Payson-area utility extension, on-site treatment, and water-rights diligence.

Lead: Civil engineering + utility districts
03

Access & Roads

Upgrading Granite Dells Rd access and internal roads to resort standard, coordinated with the County and any Forest Service interface.

Lead: Civil + County Public Works
04

Payson & Community

Though in the County, the project lives in Payson's economy. Early alignment with the Town on services, lodging tax, and jobs builds the political goodwill any resort needs.

Lead: Founding partners + Town of Payson
05

The Arcis Corridor

Rim Club & Chaparral Pines (Arcis) are next door. A reciprocal golf / membership arrangement turns a neighbor into an amenity — and a marketing partner.

Lead: Partnership development
06

Brand & Operator

A flag (or independent-luxury collection) and a proven operator de-risk financing and accelerate the ramp. The model above supports either path.

Lead: Founding partners + capital
The Partnership

How we might build it together.

We've left this open by design. The land may qualify as up to ~$6.5M of already-contributed equity to aid the raise — the question is simply how the Edson family wants to participate in what it becomes.

Land for Equity

Build together

The Edsons contribute the land as their stake in a joint venture; our team brings the development, the capital raise, and operations. Greatest upside, shared risk.

Ground Lease

Own & collect

Retain ownership and lease to the venture for long-term, structured income — plus reversion of a built resort. Lower risk, steady return.

Sale or Option

Clean exit

A purchase or option at fair value if the family prefers certainty today over participation tomorrow. We're open to the right structure.

A capital plan — debt / equity split, raise size, and partner economics — accompanies whichever direction the conversation takes. The model above frames the order of magnitude.

The Mission

Bret — the same eighty acres can raise more than a resort. It can raise people. We propose a Christian vocational school where students learn a real trade, graduate with little or no debt, and walk into jobs the country is desperate to fill.

It is a ministry at heart and a business by design — deliberately for-profit so it never depends on the next donation to keep its doors open. Tuition, the work students produce, and housing carry the school; faith and craftsmanship carry the students.

They won't practice on scrap lumber. They'll build the campus itself — shops, cabins, and a stone chapel that will stand for a hundred years — and take on real projects for the community and for the resort next door.

Sears · Gaynor · Heiler Faith, work, and a trade that lasts.
